# Nonsense Podcast Production Workflow

Status: Current workflow as described by Jake on 2026-08-21. This is a working record, not yet reviewed by CJ or Jeff.

## Core operating constraint

Every episode is a battle against the clock. Topic selection, research, writing, and the final outline must all account for the fixed recording length. Recurring show segments consume part of that runtime before the main material begins.

## Headlines episode

### Editorial funnel

1. CJ and Jeff begin with approximately 20 possible headline topics.
2. They sort the pool and reduce it to approximately 10 topics.
3. They discuss and sort the topics again, arriving at a working set of approximately 10.
4. They research those 10 topics.
5. They argue over the researched topics and reduce the set to five.
6. They write about the five as if each might appear on the show.
7. They reduce the five to the final three.
8. They complete the writing and preparation for those three topics.
9. They record the episode with those three headlines.

Open question: Jake described step 3 as “get another 10.” Confirm whether this means refining the existing 10, replacing them with a new 10, or adding another set of 10.

### Recurring segments

- The “4-3-2”: a joke used as the cold open.
- A short update about CJ's week.
- A short update about Jeff's week.
- Three final headline stories.
- A short advertisement or feature about an old technology item at the end.

### Practical implication

The usable runtime for the three headline stories is the total target length minus the time required for the 4-3-2, both weekly updates, transitions, and the old-technology ending.

## Big Story episode

### Topic selection

1. CJ and Jeff each maintain a list of Big Story topics they want to cover.
2. Ownership loosely alternates between Jeff and CJ from week to week.
3. They debate which topic should be selected for the episode.

### Research and outlining

1. CJ and Jeff conduct substantial independent research.
2. When they come together, they combine their separate research.
3. They decide which facts, arguments, examples, and sections matter most.
4. They build an outline tailored to that week's topic; the structure can change from episode to episode.
5. They assign or negotiate time across the outline and cut lower-priority material to fit the episode clock.
6. They record the episode from the prioritized, time-boxed outline.

### Recurring ending

- CJ and Jeff each give a recommendation, usually a piece of media related to the episode topic.

## Observed friction to investigate

These are hypotheses to validate with CJ and Jeff, not established facts:

- Research effort may be spent on headline candidates that are later discarded.
- Writing five headline candidates in depth before selecting three may create avoidable work.
- Independent Big Story research may overlap or be difficult to merge quickly.
- Runtime pressure may be addressed too late, after too much material has already been prepared.
- General-purpose AI may generate prose without understanding the selection funnel, recurring segments, or clock.

## Current project boundary

- Focus on Seasons 3 and 4.
- Speaker-level structuring is not required for the first useful product.
- Preserve source text and uncertainty; do not invent speaker attribution.
- Treat fine-tuning as an optional later step, not a prerequisite.
